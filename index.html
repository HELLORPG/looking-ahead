Modeling Future Evolution in Autoregressive Video Generation

Learning from what lies ahead.Generative future prediction helps models learn world evolution.

01 / THE IDEA

To generate the next moment, learn to see beyond it.

Extending a video does not necessarily mean learning how a scene evolves. Videos contain strong temporal redundancy: neighboring chunks often share similar appearance and motion. Under teacher forcing, a model can exploit this local continuity in the ground-truth history to denoise the current chunk. This creates a shortcut with limited pressure to learn the underlying scene dynamics.

A natural way to move beyond this shortcut is to look farther into the future. As prediction targets move farther from the observed history, local continuity provides less direct support. Predicting these future chunks without the intervening ground-truth context places greater pressure on the model to capture temporal progression. The auxiliary task uses future video chunks as generative targets in latent space, without an additional semantic teacher.

02 / FUTURE CHUNK PREDICTION

Training-time supervision

Predicting future chunks. Learning visual evolution.

FCP adds parallel future predictors to an autoregressive video generator. They use intermediate backbone features to predict future chunks at different temporal offsets, without observing the intervening chunks.

METHOD DETAILS

Predict in parallel. Each future offset has a separate predictor conditioned directly on the same source representation. The branches do not exchange hidden states, preventing visual cues from noisy targets at one offset from reaching predictors at other offsets. This reduces visual shortcuts and information leakage.

Single-chunk conditioning. Auxiliary predictors are conditioned only on the hidden states of the source chunk, rather than the full history of hidden states. This promotes compact predictive representations while reducing auxiliary prediction costs.

Dual-source supervision. Clean latent processing encodes historical context for subsequent generation, while noisy latent processing supports current-chunk synthesis through denoising. Both roles are essential to autoregressive generation, so we apply future-prediction supervision to both types of representations.

Training-only modules. Future-prediction losses backpropagate through source representations to supervise the generator backbone. At inference, all FCP-specific modules are removed, preserving the teacher-forcing baseline’s autoregressive rollout with no additional overhead.
